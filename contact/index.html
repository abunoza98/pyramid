    Why Football Pyramids exists

    League tables are usually shown one division at a time. Football Pyramids puts a country's divisions on one page, top flight first, so you can see how they connect: who goes up, who comes down, and where any club sits in the wider pyramid.

    Where the tables come from

    Tables, fixtures and results come from a commercial football data provider.

    • Standings are checked hourly through the afternoon and evening (UK time) and once in the morning. Each table says which date it is as of: the last time it changed, so a table can show an older date after a quiet week without anything being wrong.
    • Fixtures and kick-off times are checked every two hours. Postponed, cancelled and "time to be confirmed" games are marked as such, and a moved kick-off shows its old time for three weeks.
    • Live scores update about every minute while games are on, and the tables show how they stand until the official tables catch up.

    How the tables are worked out

    • Positions are as published, which follows each league's own tie-break rules.
    • The feed doesn't list points deductions, so we work them out from wins, draws and points and note each one under its table.
    • Promotion, play-off and relegation places are set for each league from its competition rules. Where places change from year to year (European spots, for example), they're taken from the data provider's labels instead.
    • For past seasons, where the provider's final table stopped short of the last matchday, we rebuilt it from the results where we could; a few are still being checked by hand. Seasons cut short by Covid are shown as they ended.
